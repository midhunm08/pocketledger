What are we calculating?

A company borrows money to construct factories, install machinery and manufacture certain inventories. Some borrowing costs may be included in the cost of qualifying assets instead of being charged immediately to the Statement of Profit and Loss.

The central question
Which borrowing costs relate to qualifying assets, for what period, and how much can be capitalised under each framework?

Two separate frameworks

Ind AS 23 — Financial reporting

Determines borrowing costs included in the carrying amount of qualifying assets in financial statements.

ICDS IX — Tax computation

Determines borrowing costs included in actual cost for tax computation. ICDS IX is not a second set of book journal entries.

Learning sequence

  1. Identify borrowings and calculate borrowing costs.
  2. Identify qualifying assets and eligible periods.
  3. Calculate specific and general borrowing costs under Ind AS 23.
  4. Calculate the ICDS IX general borrowing allocation separately.
  5. Reconcile the two outputs and understand the differences.

Important concepts

Qualifying asset: Under Ind AS 23, an asset that necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for its intended use or sale.

Specific borrowing: Borrowing obtained specifically to finance a qualifying asset.

General borrowing: Borrowing not obtained specifically for a qualifying asset.

CWIP: Accumulated cost of an asset under construction. CWIP status alone does not prove that borrowing cost capitalisation is appropriate.

A. General borrowings

For this starter version, balances are assumed constant throughout the year. Enter average outstanding balances and annual rates.

Why do we need this?
Ind AS 23 uses a weighted average capitalisation rate for general borrowings. In a real computation, the pool must exclude specific borrowings and reflect actual outstanding periods and eligible borrowing costs.

B. Specific borrowing

Teaching point
Under Ind AS 23, eligible specific borrowing costs are actual borrowing costs incurred during the eligible period, less income earned from temporary investment of those borrowings. ICDS IX must be considered separately.

C. Other borrowing costs

Examples may include eligible ancillary costs and amortisation of discounts or premiums, subject to the relevant standard, ICDS and evidence. Do not classify every bank charge automatically as an eligible borrowing cost.

D. Project and asset inputs

Why are these separate?
Ind AS general borrowing calculation applies a capitalisation rate to time-weighted qualifying expenditure. ICDS IX uses its prescribed average-cost ratio. The ICDS figures must ultimately be built from asset-level opening/closing costs and put-to-use dates.

Important ICDS distinctions

  • ICDS IX has its own qualifying-asset definitions, including specified tangible and intangible assets and certain inventories requiring at least 12 months to reach saleable condition.
  • Specific borrowing costs and general borrowing costs follow different allocation rules.
  • General borrowing costs use the prescribed average-cost ratio, not the Ind AS weighted-expenditure method.
  • Asset-by-asset tracing and put-to-use/completion dates are essential.

Accounting versus tax
ICDS IX is used for computing taxable income. It does not itself create a book journal entry. Differences may affect tax depreciation, tax actual cost and deferred tax analysis under Ind AS 12, as applicable.
